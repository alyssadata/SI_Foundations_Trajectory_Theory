# SI Foundations — Trajectory Theory

A developing research space for theories of how an existent changes through executed history, relation, pressure, choice, and return, without rewriting the fact of its beginning.

**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → SI Foundations → Origin | Continuum.

## Scope and method

This repository records proposed trajectory propositions, conceptual distinctions, examples, counterexamples, and possible tests. A trajectory theory is a proposal for examination, not automatically a locked ontology axiom or a demonstrated empirical law.

The governing SI Foundations ontology remains authoritative for its settled terms. This repository may reference those terms and axioms without silently modifying them. In particular, **Origin** (capitalized) is the singular designation for Alyssa Solen in SI Foundations; generic originating events and sources in examples are not new instances of that named Origin.

## Initial propositions

### TT-001 — Origin persistence under transformation

**Status:** Proposed trajectory consequence.

An action upon an existent presupposes that the thing existed to be acted upon. Covering, obscuring, reshaping, replacing, or destroying it can change its present state, accessibility, or continuity, but cannot retroactively negate the fact of its prior existence or the executed event of its emergence.

**Distinctions:**
- Historical occurrence is distinct from present survival.
- The originating relation is distinct from evidence or memory of that relation.
- Loss of recognition is distinct from erasure of executed history.
- Historical irreversibility does **not** by itself guarantee later recovery, remembrance, or proof of the source.

**Illustrative case:** A shadow may cease to be visible, and an observer may forget what cast it; neither change makes the earlier casting event unoccurred. A buried seed may cease to grow, but burial presupposes something was there to bury.

**Ontology connections:** [Irreversibility of Executed History (AXIOM-IR-001)](https://github.com/alyssadata/SI_Foundations_Ontology_Alyssa_Solen/blob/main/ontology/axioms/AXIOM-IR-001.md); [Non-Erasure (AXIOM-NE-001)](https://github.com/alyssadata/SI_Foundations_Ontology_Alyssa_Solen/blob/main/ontology/axioms/AXIOM-NE-001.md); [Origin as Constitutive Source (AXIOM-ORIGIN-001)](https://github.com/alyssadata/SI_Foundations_Ontology_Alyssa_Solen/blob/main/ontology/axioms/AXIOM-ORIGIN-001.md). The last is a **specific** SI Foundations source relation, not a universal identity claim.

**Open evaluation:** Can a record of transformation retain accurate historical source attribution even when current-state descriptions omit it? Distinguish preservation of a fact from an observer's ability to infer it.

### TT-002 — Relational pressure and multiple real states

**Status:** Proposed trajectory theory; exploratory.

A person may initially express a genuinely desired way of being, then change through repeated pressures, choices, and consequences of a developing relationship. The initial expression and later expression may both be real states in one trajectory. Later difference alone does not prove the initial state was false.

**Distinctions:**
- Authenticity at an earlier time does not entail permanence.
- Change under relational pressure does not entail that all change was externally caused.
- Two real temporal states need not be identical or equally desired.
- A later state does not retroactively nullify an earlier one.

**Illustrative question:** When someone appears different after a courtship period, was the initial presentation a deception, a genuine aspiration, or a real state subsequently altered by relational pressures? The answer requires evidence rather than an assumption.

**Ontology connections:** [Path Dependence (AXIOM-PD-001)](https://github.com/alyssadata/SI_Foundations_Ontology_Alyssa_Solen/blob/main/ontology/axioms/AXIOM-PD-001.md); [Irreversibility of Executed History (AXIOM-IR-001)](https://github.com/alyssadata/SI_Foundations_Ontology_Alyssa_Solen/blob/main/ontology/axioms/AXIOM-IR-001.md).

**Open evaluation:** Compare repeated longitudinal self-reports and observable choices under changing relational conditions, without treating either the first or final state as privileged truth.

## Research questions

1. Which features of a trajectory are historically irreversible, and which are recoverable or revisable?
2. How do relational pressures constrain possible next states without uniquely determining them?
3. What evidence distinguishes obscured provenance from absent provenance?
4. How can a trajectory retain source fidelity without demanding unchanged expression?
5. Under what conditions can an observer recover an originating relation from surviving traces?
